# Sugarcane Agronomy & Field Establishment Reference Guide
Source: Compiled Agronomic Extension Guidelines for Sugarcane Production (Educational Reference)

## 1. Planting and Germination Dynamics
Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum) is vegetatively propagated using stem cuttings known as setts or seed pieces, typically containing two to three viable buds (eyes). Optimal soil conditions for germination and primary shoot emergence include:
- Soil temperature between 26°C and 33°C (temperatures below 20°C significantly retard sprouting).
- Adequate soil moisture (approximately 60-70% field capacity).
- Row spacing typically ranging from 0.9 m to 1.5 m (single row) or dual-row configurations (1.8 m centers).

Primary shoot emergence begins 10 to 25 days after planting under favorable conditions. Tillering (stooling) commences roughly 40 to 60 days after planting, forming the primary stool structure that determines initial stalk population density.

## 2. Defining Canopy Gaps in Sugarcane Stands
A gap in an early-stage sugarcane stand is defined as a continuous linear distance along a planted row where no viable sugarcane shoots emerge or survive.
- Standard Operational Definition: A gap is generally categorized when a blank space exceeds 50 cm (0.5 meters) in high-density planting regimes, or exceeds 1.0 meter in conventional standard-spacing fields.
- Micro-gaps (< 30 cm) are often naturally compensated by compensatory tillering from adjacent vigorous stools during the tillering and grand growth phases.
- Critical Gaps (> 1.0 meter): These gaps fail to achieve full canopy closure, resulting in permanent population deficit, weed competition within the vacant furrow, sunlight wastage, and reduced cane tonnage at harvest.

## 3. Causes of Poor Germination and Gap Formation
Gaps are seldom uniform and occur due to multifaceted biotic and abiotic stresses:
1. Poor Sett Quality: Use of immature top setts, over-mature dry stalks, diseased bud eyes (e.g., infected with red rot Colletotrichum falcatum or smut Sporisorium scitamineum), or mechanical injury during sett cutting.
2. Soil Compaction and Waterlogging: In poorly drained furrows, saturated anaerobic conditions cause sett rotting (pineapple disease, Ceratocystis paradoxa).
3. Moisture Stress / Drought: Planting into dry furrows without immediate germination irrigation desiccates bud eyes.
4. Depth of Planting Errors: Setts buried deeper than 7-10 cm suffer from delayed emergence or asphyxiation, while setts buried too shallow (< 2 cm) dry out rapidly.
5. Pests: Early-stage damage by termites (Odontotermes obesus), white grubs (Holotrichia serrata), or early shoot borer (Chilo infuscatellus) killing the primary growing shoot ('dead heart').

## 4. Agronomic Thresholds and Replanting Decision Matrix
Decisions regarding remedial field interventions must be grounded in precise ground verification:
- Acceptable Stand: Total gap length under 10% to 15% of the total row length. No inter-row replanting required; compensatory tillering will cover the canopy.
- Moderate Gap (15% - 25% total row gap): Targeted gap-filling (gap planting) recommended within 30 to 45 days after initial planting.
- Severe Failure (> 30% gap frequency): Stand viability is compromised. Complete plough-out and replanting may be economically necessary, as delayed infilling results in severe shading of younger replants by older shoots.

## 5. Timing for Field Intervention
Gap filling must be executed early, ideally between 25 and 35 days after planting (DAP). Interventions delayed beyond 45 DAP are prone to stunted development due to light competition and apical dominance from the primary canopy. Pre-germinated polybag seedlings, bud-chip seedlings, or single-bud setts nurtured in nurseries provide the highest survival and synchronized maturity.
